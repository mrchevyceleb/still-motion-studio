# Real engines now power simple animation

**Genuine learned interpolation now works in Still through the installed Cascadeur application and through local CondMDI inference.** Both adapters infer compatible humanoid motion, then return rotations to the imported character while preserving its original skin and bone offsets. Both completed the four-second demo and an 18.2-second, 119-bone Mixamo clip with all six authored poses preserved exactly. A separate MuJoCo provider supplies actual articulated dynamics correction through numerical optimization. That correction performed well on the demo, but the full Mixamo test retained substantial physical errors and must remain a reviewable proposal. The remaining gaps are reliable motion quality across arbitrary rigs and commercial clearance for the optional pretrained model. ## The installed Cascadeur engine avoids re-rigging the source

Cascadeur 2026.2 exposes MCP/Python integration and its public interpolation enum includes `AI`. Still's adapter uses those supported interfaces with a temporary copy of the installed Cascy sample, transfers pose deltas onto that compatible rig, invokes the **actual installed learned model**, and retargets the result. The original imported mesh, skin bindings, hierarchy, and non-root offsets stay in Still. No proprietary weights or binary code are copied into the editor. This proxy route supersedes the earlier unsuccessful direct quick-rig experiment. ([Cascadeur release notes](https://cascadeur.com/help/category/319), [public AI enum](https://cascadeur.com/python-api/_generate/csc.layers.layer.Interpolation.html), [native integration evidence](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/research_notes/Real motion engines/native-cascadeur.md>))

The completed proof returned **121 frames for the demo and 547 for Mixamo**, preserving all six source anchors exactly; maximum quaternion normalization error was below 5×10⁻¹⁶ and non-root offset change was zero. Those checks establish successful inference and structural preservation, not artistic quality. The provider requires the separately installed application and enabled local scripting server, supports humanoids, and currently caps jobs at 20 seconds. Cascadeur limits adjacent native keys to 120 frames; longer source gaps are inferred within that limit and retimed with a warning. Unusual proportions and long-gap timing still need visual review. ([Proof measurements](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/cascadeur-proof/verification.json>), [official inbetweening constraints](https://cascadeur.com/help/category/278), [adapter](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/backend/cascadeur.py>))

## CondMDI offers a separate learned runtime

CondMDI directly conditions a motion diffusion model on sparse authored poses, making it a strong match for six-pose editing. The installed provider uses the **authors' random-frames checkpoint**, with MoLab's feature codecs and model implementation. All 196 learned tensors match the author's averaged checkpoint bit-for-bit. Seed and conditioning tests established stochastic, context-sensitive motion in unobserved frames; this is learned inference, not a relabeled interpolation curve. ([CondMDI authors](https://github.com/setarehc/diffusion-motion-inbetweening), [MoLab](https://github.com/JasonNero/MoLab), [checkpoint provenance](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/ai-motion/primary-provenance.json>), [conditioning proof](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/ai-motion/conditioning-proof.json>))

On this RTX 4090, the demo took **10.1 seconds including cold loading**, and Mixamo took **16.1 seconds**, with about 1.37 GB peak PyTorch allocation. Both retained exact authored poses and bone lengths within 7×10⁻¹⁶ m. The model uses 22 canonical human joints at 20 fps; longer clips use overlapping inference windows, including three for the Mixamo proof. The current adapter permits up to 60 seconds, but long-window coherence, unusual proportions, and contacts still require review. ([Measured round trips](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/ai-motion/roundtrip-proof.json>), [implementation limits](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/docs/neural-motion.md>))

**MIT code licensing does not establish commercial rights to these pretrained weights and all training data.** The model remains a separate local download; author confirmation or an explicitly cleared replacement is a release requirement. DART deserves follow-up because its license explicitly covers code and model under Apache 2.0, subject to third-party terms. Its SMPL-oriented integration and actual constrained-inference performance have not been tested here. ([CondMDI license scope](https://github.com/setarehc/diffusion-motion-inbetweening#license), [DART license](https://github.com/zkf1997/DART/blob/main/LICENSE), [DART implementation](https://github.com/zkf1997/DART))

## MuJoCo supplies real physics with visible limits

Cascadeur documents AutoPhysics as physics-based correction with force, torque, friction, ballistic, and preservation constraints. Its existence does not require every independent equivalent feature to use a neural network. MuJoCo provides articulated inverse dynamics and frictional contact forces, so Still's separate **Physics correction** provider optimizes the motion against an actual dynamics engine. It measures the unsupported external force and torque that the root would require, alongside foot penetration, contact sliding, joint torque, and anchor drift. This is an independent implementation with approximate body masses and collision proxies, not a recreation of Cascadeur's solver. ([Cascadeur AutoPhysics](https://cascadeur.com/help/tools/physics_tools/autophysics), [corrector constraints](https://cascadeur.com/help/category/292), [MuJoCo computation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation.html), [implementation](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/backend/physics.py>))

On the 121-frame demo, **unphysical root-force RMS fell from 686.7 N to 4.31 N** in 24 seconds; maximum foot compression was 0.658 mm and sliding was 0.269 mm/s. A separate airborne test reduced root-force RMS from 687.8 N to 16.4 N as optimization produced a gravity-consistent trajectory. The harder full Mixamo clip exposed the limit: force improved from 2,240 N to 1,220 N, but sliding worsened from 0.196 to 0.241 m/s and compression reached 7.08 mm. The output correctly carries `requiresReview: true`; these residuals are too large to describe the result as physically feasible. Sparse solutions that regress beyond the acceptance thresholds are rejected after full-frame measurement. ([Demo result](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/physics-proof/demo-dense-output.json>), [flight result](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/physics-proof/flight-output.json>), [full Mixamo result](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/physics-proof/mixamo-full-output.json>), [rejection verification](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/.local/physics-proof/verified-results.json>))

## Better contact and rig calibration matter next

The immediate physics work is a smaller, better calibrated rigid-body model for imported rigs: infer anatomical axes and masses, improve contact timing, handle duplicated helper bones, and validate long sequences. Current limits are conservative joint cones; full-body self-collision and accurate muscle limits are absent. Named quadruped supports are accepted by the independent solver, but **quadruped quality is unbenchmarked and neither learned provider supplies validated quadruped inference**. These are substantial product gaps even though editing and rig preservation already work. ([Physics limitations](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/research_notes/Real motion engines/physical-correction.md>), [Cascadeur humanoid scope](https://cascadeur.com/help/category/278))

Native AutoPhysics calculation was observed, but unattended baking is not integrated: its public Snap action opens an all-frames/keyframes choice, and invoking the action alone changed no source joints. PHC is a credible future learned physics controller with downloadable checkpoints, but its standard setup uses Isaac Gym and SMPL-specific processing; it has not been run here or adapted to arbitrary FBX rigs. PhysDiff is a relevant research method, but the reviewed author page did not establish a complete drop-in checkpoint/runtime. Neither is a ready replacement for the working local dynamics provider. ([Native bake evidence](<C:/Users/MattJ/Documents/ChatGPT/Animation Rigger/research_notes/Real motion engines/native-cascadeur.md>), [Snap release change](https://cascadeur.com/help/category/319), [PHC authors' repository](https://github.com/ZhengyiLuo/PHC), [PhysDiff authors' project](https://nvlabs.github.io/PhysDiff/))

## Conclusion

The product should make generated motion easy to compare, accept, and undo while keeping provider identity and quality evidence visible. Preserving the source skin solves the import failure that motivated the editor; preserving authored intent and producing credible contacts are separate acceptance criteria. The next useful benchmark is a small set of visibly reviewed Mixamo motions and contrasting body proportions, with measured contact and anchor errors, rather than adding another unverified engine.
